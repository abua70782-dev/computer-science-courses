History of Computer Science

Charles Babbage designed the Analytical Engine, a mechanical general-purpose computer, while Ada Lovelace wrote the first algorithm intended to be processed by a machine, making her the world's first computer programmer. A century later, in the 1930s and 1940s, Alan Turing formalized the mathematical foundations of computation with the concept of the Turing Machine, which proved that a single machine could perform any computable task.
This theoretical breakthrough merged with wartime engineering to produce the first electronic digital computers, such as ENIAC which used thousands of vacuum tubes to perform complex calculations at speeds previously unimaginable.
